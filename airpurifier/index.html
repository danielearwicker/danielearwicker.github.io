Why the “room at 4.8 ACH” column exists

Manufacturers pick their own air-change rate when they quote a maximum room size, so “up to 294 m²” and “up to 20 m²” are not measuring the same thing — some of those headline numbers work out at barely one air change an hour. The highlighted column restates each CADR at the standard allergy-relief benchmark of 4.8 air changes per hour with a 2.4 m ceiling: room m² = CADR ÷ 11.52. That column is arithmetic done here, not a manufacturer figure. Everything else in the table is quoted as published.

Notes on individual rows

    • CADR standards differ. AHAM AC-1 (used in the US) reports three separate figures in cfm — smoke, dust and pollen. The Chinese GB/T 18801 standard reports one figure in m³/h and tends to run higher. Where a maker publishes three values, this table shows the smoke CADR, which is the most demanding, with the full set on hover.
    • Airflow is not CADR. Air throughput counts every cubic metre the fan moves, including whatever passes through unfiltered or imperfectly filtered. CADR discounts it by filtration efficiency. A maker quoting only airflow is quoting the flattering number.
    • Blanks are real. An italic “not published” means the manufacturer does not state that figure anywhere on its own site — not that it could not be found.
    • Prices are the ones Which? listed on 1 September 2026 and have already drifted on some manufacturer sites; treat them as a guide to relative cost.
